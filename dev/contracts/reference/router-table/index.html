    Router table¶

    An entry of the router table maps one router BLIS does not host, in one mode, to the policy of llm-d's router that stands closest to it, with the evidence for the match (design §11.3 and §11.4).

    The entries are in the catalog, as routers/<name>.yaml, and the catalog reference shows them. LoadRouterEntry reads one strictly, and Entry.Validate checks it. The name is the file's stem and is not a key of the file.

    routers/dynamo-round-robin.yaml
    # Dynamo's frontend in its round-robin mode, which it runs when its configuration sets none.
# The entry maps the mode to llm-d's round-robin policy and says exactly how the two differ and
# how far the choice of policy moves BLIS's figures. Every citation is a line of a router's own
# code at the commit named, or of llm-d's router at the commit the module tag v1.0.0-blis.3 of
# inference-sim/llm-d-router points to. The bound is measured on BLIS only (see the guide
# contracts/guides/router-sensitivity.md); it is a sensitivity, not an error against Dynamo.
kind: RouterMapping
router: dynamo
mode: round-robin
revisions:
  - 1682167d3c97f0fc8cd405dee51f17288eeaeb55
policy: round-robin
reason: >-
  Dynamo's round-robin mode rotates strictly per request: it takes the next value of a counter
  and indexes the list of available workers by it, so successive requests go to successive
  workers and every worker gets the same number to within one. llm-d's round-robin policy does
  not rotate: it is the max-score picker with no scorer, every endpoint is tied, and the picker
  chooses among the tied endpoints at random (the hosted scheduler shuffles the candidates before
  each pick), as the random picker does. Neither reads load, so neither reacts to a worker that
  falls behind, and over a run both spread requests over every endpoint. They differ in the
  order: Dynamo's is a cycle and llm-d's is a random draw. The nearest llm-d policy picks at
  random among tied endpoints, and the random picker is equally close, so the first group of the
  bound is against it. How far the choice of policy moves BLIS's figures is the bound: small for
  time per output token and for throughput in most of the regime, large for time to first token
  at 64 to 256 clients when the alternative reads load. An earlier version of this entry held
  that no policy was close enough because llm-d's counter, drawn at every pick of every profile,
  would give a prefill pool of even size every other endpoint. The router's own tests and BLIS
  runs do not show it (the pool-balance difference), and the argument is not made here.
difference:
  - aspect: strict-rotation
    text: >-
      Dynamo rotates strictly per request (push_router.rs 357-372): the n-th request goes to the
      instance at n modulo the number of available instances. The nearest llm-d policy picks at
      random among tied endpoints: the max-score picker rotates the tied candidates by a counter,
      but they reach it in a seeded random order in a hosted runtime, so the choice is uniformly
      random, and tests of pools of two, three and four reach every endpoint in near equal counts
      and not in a cycle. The router has no policy that rotates strictly. The random picker is
      equally close, and the first group of the bound is against it. These statements are about
      inference-sim/llm-d-router at commit 03246be994224ef031dfdafb966c54f60d01e933 and no other
      release; the policy list states none of them.
    evidence: [1, 8, 9, 10]
  - aspect: frontends
    text: >-
      A Dynamo deployment can run several frontend processes, each with router objects and
      counters of its own that start at zero, so the sequence of requests a worker sees
      interleaves independent rotations; the recipes the dashboard read run several
      (enable_multiple_frontends). BLIS hosts one router and picks at random among tied endpoints. Which frontend a request reaches is the load
      balancer's choice and is not modelled.
    evidence: [2]
  - aspect: pool-balance
    text: >-
      Under llm-d's policy a prefill pool is used in full, as the random choice among tied
      endpoints gives. With a workload that saturates
      the prefill pool (8,192 prompt tokens, 16 output tokens), round-robin served 25.3 to 27.0
      requests per second at 16 to 1,024 clients with two prefill endpoints, against 27.1 to 27.3
      under least-active-requests and 13.6 with one prefill endpoint; with four it served 46.3 to
      53.3 against 53.6 to 54.4. A pool used through one endpoint would give about half. The
      14 percent gap at four endpoints and 16 clients is also seen under the random picker and
      the default policy, so it is the arrival imbalance of a policy that ignores load.
      BLIS does not record which prefill endpoint served a request, so the
      mechanism is not confirmed. The tables are in the guide.
    evidence: [5, 6, 7, 8]
  - aspect: load-blind
    text: >-
      Neither mode reads load, so neither reacts to a worker that falls behind. A policy that
      does (least-active-requests, default, queue-scorer) is not this mode; the second group of
      ratios in the bound is what a wrong guess of the mode would cost.
    evidence: [1, 7]

  not_measured:
    - One model (minimax-m2.5) on one part (gb200-nvl72) over NIXL on ib-400g, served by vLLM 0.32.0. Another model or part moves where the pools saturate, and with it where the large ratios sit.
    - Closed-loop clients with fixed prompt and output lengths. No run had a prefix shared between requests, a session of several turns, a spread of lengths, or an open-loop arrival rate.
    - Concurrency 16, 64, 256 and 1024 only, and the pool shapes 1P3D, 2P3D, 3P2D and 4P1D only, with one-GPU prefill instances and four-GPU decode instances (tensor parallel 4 with expert parallelism). Counts between the swept points, and larger pools, were not run.
    - The ratios are policy sensitivity, not substitution error. They say how far BLIS's own figures move when the policy changes, between policies inside BLIS. No run behind Dynamo was compared with a run of BLIS, so how far Dynamo's real behaviour is from either policy is not measured.
    - The regime is exact. A point whose client count is not one of those listed, or whose model, part, engine release, fabric, instance layout or pool shape is not the one named, has no bound for this regime and stays router_unsupported; nothing is interpolated between the sampled counts, and time to first token has a sharp knee between them.
    - Time to first token, at 64 to 256 clients, is where the ratios against the policies that read load are large. The bound does not hold tightly there, and a ranking by TTFT p50 or p99 in that regime should not rest on this substitution. Time per output token and throughput move far less, except at the extremes of concurrency.

    Keys¶

    Key Type Required Meaning
    kind string yes Always RouterMapping.
    router string yes The router the run was measured behind, in lower case, for example dynamo. Never llm-d, which BLIS hosts.
    mode string yes The router's mode or algorithm as the run's configuration names it. When the configuration sets none, the mode the router's code defaults to, and a citation says so.
    session_affinity_ttl_s integer no The router's session-affinity idle timeout in seconds, when the configuration sets one. At least 1. Two runs that differ in it are different keys.
    revisions list of strings yes The builds of the router the run used for which the entry is established, each a full 40-character commit, sorted, each once. A mapping is a claim about the behaviour of a build, so an entry shown for one build is not inherited by another with the same mode: a run on a build not listed has no mapping and stays router_unsupported (design §11.4). Every router-side citation is at one of them, and each has a citation for the mode and one for the policy.
    policy string yes A policy llm-d's router offers, or none.
    reason string yes What the router's mode and the policy do alike and differently; for none, why no policy is close enough.
    difference list for a policy other than none; else no Each way the mode and the policy differ, one statement per aspect, each citing the code that shows it, below.
    bound mapping for a policy other than none; else no How far the choice of policy moves each figure on BLIS, measured, with its regime and evidence, below.
    evidence list yes Citations of both routers' code or documentation, below.

    The key of an entry is the router, the mode and the session-affinity setting. The entry's file is named <router>-<mode>, with -affinity-<seconds> when the setting is set, and the validator refuses another name, so one key cannot be stated by two files, in a catalog or in an overlay.

    Lookup by revision¶

    routertable.Resolve (and Data.LookupRouter) takes the router, the mode, the session-affinity setting and the router build the run used, and returns the entry with one of four outcomes. mapped: the entry covers the build and names a policy; only this scores the point, at G2 (router_differs). no_policy: it covers the build and says no policy is close enough. revision_not_covered: an entry has the key and does not list the build, so what it shows is not shown for this one. no_entry: no entry has the key. The last three are G5 router_unsupported. A point that relies on an entry carries the build it used in the revision of its router_differs, and Data.CheckRouterSubstitutions refuses one whose entry is missing, is another router's, does not cover the build, or maps to another policy.

    The committed dynamo-round-robin entry covers one build of Dynamo, commit 1682167d3c97f0fc8cd405dee51f17288eeaeb55 (26 April 2026), the build the April 2026 recipes pin. A run on a later Dynamo build stays router_unsupported until an entry cites it.

    Policies:

    round-robin · random-picker · least-active-requests · default · none

    evidence¶

    Key Type Required Meaning
    side string yes Whose code it is, below.
    field string yes The field of the entry it supports, below.
    repository string yes <owner>/<name> on GitHub. On the llm-d side, llm-d/llm-d-router or inference-sim/llm-d-router; on the router side, not those.
    commit string yes The full 40-character commit the passage is read at. A branch or a tag is not a pinned revision.
    path string yes The file, from the repository's root. For a policy other than none, one llm-d citation shows the behaviour the policy is matched to, at the commit it names; the contract keeps no list of files.
    lines string yes A line number, or a range <first>-<last>, at commit.
    claim string yes What the passage shows.

    Sides:

    router · llm-d

    router
    The router the run used.
    llm-d
    llm-d's router. It supports the policy only.

    Fields:

    mode · session_affinity_ttl_s · policy

    Each field the entry states has a citation (SUB-18): the router's own code for its mode and, when set, its session_affinity_ttl_s; and, for the policy, a passage of the router that shows the behaviour it is matched to and a passage of llm-d's router that shows the policy does it.

    difference¶

    Key Type Required Meaning
    aspect string yes What differs, as a lower-case slug, for example counter, tie-break or scale. Each aspect once.
    text string yes How the router's mode and the policy differ in that aspect, and what BLIS does instead. It states a fact of the code, or says that an effect is not measured.
    evidence list of integers yes The positions (from 0) in the entry's evidence list of the citations the statement rests on. At least one, each once.

    A point record carries the same statements without evidence, because a position means nothing outside the entry:

    Key Type Required Meaning
    aspect string yes What differs, as above.
    text string yes How the router's mode and the policy differ in that aspect.

    A mapping is a claim of nearness, so the entry says how near: every way the two differ that the code shows, and for each the passages that show it. A point scored with the entry copies the statements (without the positions, which mean nothing outside the entry) into its router_differs substitution.

    bound¶

    A bound is a sensitivity, not an error. It says how far BLIS's own prediction of a figure moves when the policy changes, so it says what a wrong guess of the router's mode could cost. It does not say how far BLIS is from the router's real behaviour, which no run measured.

    A bound is measured on BLIS, so it is a fact about the two policies and the workload, not about a build of the router. The entry states it once for every build in its revisions, as it states the differences, which are facts of the code at those builds. A difference or a bound that holds at one build only would need an entry of its own, which the key (router, mode, affinity) does not allow yet.

    Key Type Required Meaning
    evidence mapping yes The measurement the ratios come from, below.
    ratios list yes The limits: at least one, each for one figure, one set of alternatives and one regime.
    not_measured list of strings yes What the bound does not cover, one sentence each. At least one: a bound that claims to cover everything is not a measured bound.
    Key Type Required Meaning
    file string yes The CSV of runs, from the repository's root. The ratios are recomputed from it.
    sha256 string yes The SHA-256 of the file, 64 lower-case hex digits, so a ratio cites the bytes it was read from.
    producer string yes <owner>/<name> of the repository BLIS was built from for the runs.
    commit string yes The full 40-character commit of the producer.
    command string yes The command that reproduces the file.
    Key Type Required Meaning
    metric string yes The metric registry identifier of the figure, for example ttft_p50_ms.
    against list of strings yes The alternatives the policy was compared with, each by the name of its plugin composition in the evidence file's policy column. Sorted, each once, none the entry's own policy.
    regime mapping yes Where the ratio was measured, below.
    max_ratio number yes The largest, over every cell of the regime and every alternative, of the larger of the two means over the smaller, each mean taken over the seeds. At least 1, rounded up to two decimals so it never understates.
    Key Type Required Meaning
    workload string yes The kind of workload, below.
    concurrencies list of integers yes The closed-loop client counts the cells ran, ascending, each once, each at least 1. A point matches only a count in the list: nothing is interpolated between two, because time to first token has a sharp knee between them.
    model string yes The catalog model the runs used.
    hardware string yes The catalog hardware the runs used.
    engine_version string yes The engine release the runs simulated, as the scenario's engine_version states it.
    fabric string yes The catalog network between the pools, the scenario's cluster.fabric.
    prefill mapping yes The parallelism of one prefill instance: tp, pp, dp (each at least 1) and expert_parallel.
    decode mapping yes The same for one decode instance.
    shapes list of strings yes The pool shapes the cells cover, each <n>P<m>D: n prefill and m decode instances. Sorted, each once.

    Regime of a point¶

    Key Type Required Meaning
    workload string yes The workload kind of the point.
    concurrency integer yes Its closed-loop client count. At least 1.
    model string yes The catalog model it simulates.
    hardware string yes The catalog hardware.
    engine_version string yes The engine release the scoring simulator runs.
    fabric string yes The catalog network between the pools.
    shape string yes Its pool shape, <n>P<m>D.
    prefill mapping yes The parallelism of one prefill instance, as for a regime.
    decode mapping yes The same for one decode instance.

    A router_differs substitution carries this regime, and the part of the entry's bound that covers it (Bound.For): load.Data.CheckRouterSubstitutions recomputes that part from the entry and refuses a bound that omits a covering ratio, carries one for another regime, or is carried for a point the entry has no bound for.

    Key Type Required Meaning
    tp integer yes Tensor-parallel width of one instance. At least 1.
    pp integer yes Pipeline-parallel width. At least 1.
    dp integer yes Data-parallel width. At least 1.
    expert_parallel boolean yes Whether the instance splits its experts across its ranks.

    Workload kinds:

    balanced · prefill-bound · decode-bound

    balanced
    Closed loop, every request 1,024 prompt tokens and 1,024 output tokens, no prefix shared between requests.
    prefill-bound
    Closed loop, 8,192 prompt tokens and 16 output tokens.
    decode-bound
    Closed loop, 256 prompt tokens and 2,048 output tokens.

    A regime is exact. Entry.BoundFor takes the point's workload, client count, model, hardware, engine release, fabric, instance layouts and pool shape and returns the ratios whose regime contains all of them; with none, the outcome is regime_not_covered and the point stays router_unsupported with the reason "no bound for this regime". A point record that carries a router bound whose regime does not list the point's client count is refused. The ratios are the sensitivity of BLIS's own figures to the choice of policy, not the error of standing a policy in for the router.

    The list is closed because a bound is a statement about the workloads it names and no others: a kind joins it with the measurement that covers it. How the ratios are measured, how to reproduce them and what they leave out is in the router sensitivity guide.

    What the validator refuses¶

    An entry with no evidence; one that lacks a citation for a field it states, or for either router's side of the policy; a policy llm-d's router does not offer; a none or a match with no reason; the router llm-d; an llm-d citation of another repository, a router citation of llm-d's, or an llm-d citation that names no file defining the policy; a file name that is not the entry's key; no revisions, a revision that is not a full commit, repeated or out of order, a router citation at a commit that is not one of the revisions, and a revision with no router citation of its own for the mode or the behaviour; a citation whose commit is not a full commit, whose path climbs out of the repository or whose lines run backward; a citation repeated.

    For a policy other than none: no difference; an aspect that is empty, not a slug or stated twice; a statement with no text or no citation, or one that names a position outside the evidence list or names one twice; no bound; a bound with no ratios, no not_measured, a file path that climbs, a hash that is not 64 lower-case hex digits, a producer that is not <owner>/<name>, a commit that is not a full commit or no command; a ratio whose metric is not in the metric registry, that names no alternative or its own policy, whose alternatives are unsorted or repeated, whose value is not a finite number of at least 1 with two decimals, whose workload is not a kind above, whose client counts are empty, below 1 or not ascending, whose model, hardware, engine release, fabric or either instance layout is missing, whose shapes are missing, malformed or unsorted; a ratio stated twice for one regime. For none: a difference or a bound, because there is no stand-in policy to differ from or to bound. The catalog's tests also recompute every ratio from the evidence file and refuse one that is not the file's.
